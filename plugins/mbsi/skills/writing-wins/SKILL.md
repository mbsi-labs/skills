# Writing wins

Readers scan, pay attention to what is simple, and stay while a question is open. These are the features that held up in our tests, and the decision each section serves. Use them for the kind of piece named in why-this-piece.

## Sections, and the reader's decision each one serves

- Headline: do I start? Promise a gain specific enough to beat the next headline.
- Lede: was the promise real? Pay the headline in the first screen.
- Nut graf: is this about me? Tie the piece to the reader's own stakes, and open several questions.
- Body, most important first: keep going, or leave? A reader who leaves anywhere still got the core.
- Subheadings: where do I re-enter? Say what the reader gets there.
- Evidence: do I believe it? Concrete proof the reader can check.
- Limits: can I trust the rest? One concrete limit, said once.
- What this says about how we work: the organization is the case study. Every kind.
- Take it: what do I do now? The first step itself, not a promise of one.

## Wins, and the kinds they fit

- Write for the person reading, about what they care about. Customer posts and research. A showpiece is about our work on purpose; write it generously, as a demonstration.
- Write like the writers that reader already reads. Customer posts.
- Keep paragraphs short. Every kind.
- Open several questions early. Keep at least one open all the way through. Answer every one by the end. Every kind.
- Name a concrete thing the reader could lose. Customer posts and decision research.
- Show the obvious fix failing before the real one. Every kind.
- Do the math for the reader: "8 times as often", not "8 of 10 instead of 1". Every kind.
- The user is the skilled one: no confession, no self-deprecation, no repeated hedge. Every kind.

Before drafting, read the exemplar of your kind in `references/` (showpiece, customer, research) and check the draft against `references/failure-cases.md` before handing it back.
