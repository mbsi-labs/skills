## The answer

Content gets found through two different machines, and most businesses run only one of them.

- **Search catches demand that already exists. Social creates demand where there was none.** Social explores for what people want; search then captures the winners.
- **Every piece of content serves one stage of a buyer's readiness**: discovering you, trusting you, or deciding to buy. Asking one piece to do all three is the most common failure.
- **Volume matters, with diminishing returns.** In Buffer's study of over 2 million Instagram posts, 3 to 5 posts a week was the sweet spot for reach and follower growth.[@1]
- **One clear topic per account wins.** Since 10 December 2025, Instagram users can see the topics the algorithm thinks they like and remove them in one tap.[@2] An account that mixes topics is easier to remove.
- **The first seconds carry the scent.** People forage for information the way animals forage for food, and follow cues that promise the most value for the least effort.[@3]

## What it did for a business stuck at $500k

Marley Baird Media sold one offer: a $50,000 done-for-you YouTube package. It had flatlined at $500,000 a year. The founders thought they needed better ad management. The ads were running and the traffic was arriving. Nothing converted, because cold traffic was meeting a $50,000 offer with nothing in between.

We built the missing middle as a value ladder: a book, a video course, live group coaching for cohorts of eight at about $8,000 each, then the $50,000 package at the top. The lower rungs didn't turn directly into $50,000 clients. They paid for the advertising, and by the time a serious buyer arrived, Marley's work was everywhere they looked. The $50,000 bookings quadrupled, and at its height the business was running more than $100,000 a month in ad spend, profitably.[@4]

The model's funnel stages come from running campaigns at scale before that: at Willowbrook Chrysler, discovery spots on the Rogers Arena jumbotron during Canucks games, mid-funnel display, and bottom-funnel ads for people who already knew they wanted a Ram.[@4]

## The five parts, and the evidence for each

**1. Explore, then exploit.** Treat social as exploration, a search for what people respond to, and the website as exploitation, catching the demand that's been found. This is the explore-exploit trade-off from decision theory, applied to marketing effort. A business that only explores goes viral and stays broke; one that only exploits ranks well and slowly loses relevance.

**2. One stage per piece.** Discovery content answers "who is this?", trust content answers "can they help?", and decision content answers "should I buy?". Our evidence is the Marley Baird ladder above: the missing trust stage was the whole problem.

**3. Volume, made affordable.** Volume helps, with diminishing returns.[@1] The way to afford it is to record content as interchangeable parts: openings, main points and calls to action. Ten openings, five main points and five calls to action make 250 possible videos from one recording session, and every combination is a test of which part did the work.

**4. One topic per account.** Describe each offer as a service for a specific group in a specific place: "website design for solo law practices in British Columbia", not "web design". Since December 2025, users can purge topics from their own feed,[@2] so mixed signals now cost followers as well as reach. Instagram's Trial Reels, launched in December 2024, let you show a new angle to non-followers first.[@5]

**5. Scent in the first seconds.** The opening has to promise the value the viewer is foraging for.[@3] Frequent visual changes hold attention; the right pace is a claim to test on your own audience.

## Not yet proven

These came from practice and secondary sources, and haven't been checked against a primary source:

- Slowing speech to about 0.8 seconds per word while keeping visuals fast tripled comments.
- Hyper-targeted account-based campaigns returned 33 times their spend.
- A specific brain mechanism makes viewers swipe after visual stagnation. The foraging model[@3] predicts the behaviour; the brain-level account is not established.

## What this says about how we work

We build models from campaigns we've run, then check each part against the best evidence we can find, and say plainly which parts are proven and which are still bets. That's the same work we'd do on your distribution before you spend another month's budget.

## Run the diagnostic on your own accounts

1. **Explore or exploit?** All social and no website: you're creating demand nobody captures. All website and no social: you're living off demand that's slowly shrinking.
2. **Is there a middle?** List what someone can buy or join between hearing of you and your main offer. If nothing, build one rung.
3. **Can you reach 3 to 5 posts a week?** Record one session of parts: 10 openings, 5 main points, 5 calls to action.
4. **One topic per account?** Write each account's focus as "[service] for [people] in [place]". If you can't, split it.
5. **Test before you commit.** Use Trial Reels to try a new angle on non-followers first.
