# Product demos, part 2: borrowed ideas, stakes and signs

## 3. Ideas borrowed from other directors' playbooks

From Pluviobyte's `ai-motion-director` skill (GitHub; it has no licence, so these are ideas in our words, not its text):

1. **Write one motion thesis sentence** before planning: what moving image changes from what state to what state, proving what claim. If none can be found, stop and find one.
2. **A beat graph, not pages.** Every beat names the moving object and its visible state change. A beat that only fades in a title is a slide.
3. **An anti-slide gate,** before building and before shipping. It fails a plan that is a list of pages, that is mostly title-plus-fade, or whose objects appear without changing.
4. **Generated images must carry story,** and every still must move (crop, parallax, light, layers) once it is in the film.

## 4. Stakes: put a person in it

The scene craft we have tested (the skills `cognitive-prophylactics` and `writing-wins`):

1. **Put a character in the situation,** seen by their hands, things and light.
2. **Show the cost,** so that status visibly moves, and name a concrete thing they could lose.
3. **Let the obvious fix fail on screen first.**
4. **Make it seeable, not argued.** The objects, the light and the time say "behind, dreading it", then "caught up, relieved". No caption says the feeling.
5. **Judge each shot** by what the viewer is rehearsing, whose status moves and what they would do differently.

## 5. Signs that carry a feeling without words

From a launch video for a bookkeeping tool. Reuse them where they fit.

| # | Feeling | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Behind, dreading it | Cold single light; a late clock; a cold drink with a skin on it; curled paper; a phone buzzing face down; a circled date; the work of a long day still on the table (chairs up, still working) |
| 5.2 | Someone is waiting | A text from a named person who has standing (the accountant), not a notification from an app |
| 5.3 | The obvious fix failing | The slow manual way, shown long enough to hurt (45 minutes, 4 rows typed), and a hand pulling back from the keys |
| 5.4 | Relief | The light turns warm; the drink is fresh; the lid goes on; the hand leaves the frame |
| 5.5 | Status restored | The waiting person's reply ("Perfect, thank you!"). A "Sent" alone shows no one's regard changing |
