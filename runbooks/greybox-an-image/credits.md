10. 1. **Gaze and pointing steer the viewer's attention.** A meta-analysis of 423 gaze-cueing effects found that "others' eye gaze reliably directs observers' attention" (`raw/papers/staging-mechanism-2026-10-28/pubmed-35404635.txt:29`, Psychol Bull 2021). Attention "is more powerfully captured by interfering social signals (such as others' gaze or hand orientation) than by non-biological directional cues (such as an arrow)" (`raw/papers/staging-mechanism-2026-10-28/pubmed-19565230.txt:16-18`). So a figure's gaze and pointing arm (steps 4 and 7) steer the reader's eye more strongly than arrows or other graphic devices would.
11. 2. **A twisted pose reads as an action.** In monkey STS, cells "did not respond to the sight of non-articulated static posture" but "responded vigorously to the articulated static posture that formed the end-point of the action", and not to similar postures "in a more relaxed muscular state" (`raw/papers/staging-mechanism-2026-10-28/pubmed-14527537.txt:27-32`). In people, still photographs with implied motion drive the brain's motion areas (MT/MST) more than photographs without it (`raw/papers/staging-mechanism-2026-10-28/pubmed-10769305.txt`, Kourtzi and Kanwisher 2000). Synthesis: a curved line of action and a twist between shoulders and hips (steps 5 and 6) are what make a still figure read as doing something; that is a reading of these results, not a test of stick figures.
